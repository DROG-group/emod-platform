## Purpose

You'll learn to identify when information operations are part of broader multi-domain campaigns, distinguishing coordinated hybrid threats from isolated incidents.

---

## Scenario

Your intelligence team observes: (1) cyber attacks on energy infrastructure, (2) disinformation about government corruption, (3) unusual military exercises near the border, (4) economic pressure via trade restrictions. These occurred over 6 weeks. You have:

- **Time available**: 24 hours to brief senior leadership
- **Stakes**: Assessment determines whether to escalate to national security council
- **Data access**: Reports from cyber, information, military, and economic sectors; no confirmed attribution yet
- **Constraint**: Each sector analyzed independently until now

---

## Task

Produce three outputs:

1. **Assessment**: Are these connected (hybrid campaign) or coincidental?
2. **Confidence level**: Low / Medium / High
3. **Next action**: Escalate, integrate monitoring, or treat as separate issues?

---

## Method: Hybrid Threat Detection Workflow

### Start here: Map the timeline

Chart all incidents:
- What happened when?
- Is there a sequence (cyber → information → military) or simultaneity?
- Do events seem coordinated or random?

**Strong signal**: Sequential escalation or synchronized multi-domain pressure
**Medium signal**: Temporal clustering (all within weeks)
**Weak signal**: Events happened in same month (many things happen)

### Step 2: Assess domain integration

Examine whether domains reinforce each other:
- Do information operations justify or amplify other domains? ("Cyber attack shows government incompetence")
- Does military posturing give credibility to information narratives? ("Corruption makes country weak")
- Do economic and cyber pressures share targeting logic?

**Strong signal combo**: Information operations reference other domains; timing suggests coordination
**Medium signal**: Domains could be related but also independent
**Weak signal**: Multiple things happening (correlation is not coordination)

### Step 3: Attribution overlap

Compare:
- Do different domain activities trace to same origin?
- Do tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) match known hybrid threat actors?
- Is there infrastructure overlap (cyber and information campaigns using same servers)?

**Strong signal**: Confirmed common attribution across domains
**Medium signal**: TTPs match known hybrid actors but no direct link
**Weak signal**: Different domains, different actors (might be opportunistic, not coordinated)

### Step 4: Strategic coherence

Ask:
- Do these activities advance a clear strategic objective? (Weaken government, coerce policy change, prepare for military action)
- Are there plausible non-hybrid explanations for each event?
- What would be different if this were a coordinated campaign vs coincidence?

**Critical test**: If you remove the assumption of coordination, does each event still make sense independently? If yes, coordination is not proven.

---

## Evidence Ladder

**Strong signals** (coordinated hybrid campaign likely):
- Confirmed attribution overlap across multiple domains
- Information operations explicitly reference other domain activities
- Synchronized timing across domains
- TTPs match known hybrid threat actor playbooks
- Strategic coherence: activities advance unified objective

**Medium signals** (worth integrated analysis):
- Temporal clustering of cross-domain events
- Information and other domains could be mutually reinforcing
- TTPs consistent with hybrid approaches but not conclusive
- Plausible strategic logic exists

**Weak signals** (insufficient alone):
- Multiple concerning events in same time period (things happen)
- "Feels coordinated" (complex systems generate patterns)
- Events involve same adversary (states do multiple things)
- Generic hybrid threat indicators (cyber + disinfo is common)

---

## Stop Rule

Stop hybrid threat analysis when:
- **Attribution remains siloed**: Can't confirm common origin across domains
- **Strategic logic unclear**: No coherent objective ties events together
- **Independent explanations sufficient**: Each event makes sense without assuming coordination
- **Escalation inappropriate**: Even if hybrid, current response capability is adequate

Don't force patterns. Adversaries aren't coordinating everything; sometimes separate things happen.

---

## DIM Mapping for Hybrid Context

How does hybrid threat assessment change intervention choice?

**If strong signals (confirmed hybrid campaign)**:
- **Cross-domain response**: Information interventions coordinate with diplomatic, economic, cyber responses
- **Gen 3** (Prebunking): Prepare public for multi-domain pressure, explain hybrid tactics
- **Gen 4** (Moderation): Coordinate platform enforcement with broader national security actions
- **Gen 5** (Interaction): Design information environment to reduce exploitation (resilience, not just detection)

**If medium signals (possible hybrid, unclear)**:
- **Integrated monitoring**: Combine analysis across domains to gather more evidence
- **Gen 3** (Prebunking): Educate about hybrid threats without claiming this is confirmed
- Prepare coordinated response options

**If weak signals (likely separate issues)**:
- Treat each domain independently
- Don't escalate to hybrid threat framework
- Standard sectoral responses

**Critical principle**: Hybrid threat framing should enable coordinated response, not create paralysis through overclaiming coordination.

---

## Key Takeaway

Hybrid threats are real, but not everything is hybrid. The test is not "multiple things happened" but "multiple things are demonstrably coordinated toward a unified objective." Focus on attribution overlap, strategic coherence, and domain integration. Don't use "hybrid threat" as a label for "concerning events we don't fully understand."

## Test Your Understanding

**Scenario**: In two months, your country experiences: (1) a data breach at a political party, (2) protests against a trade agreement (some violent), (3) disinformation about protest police brutality, (4) critical infrastructure cyber probes. No confirmed attribution yet.

**Question**: Is this a hybrid campaign? What evidence would confirm or refute?

### Your answer should include:

1. **Hypothesis + confidence**: Hybrid campaign, opportunistic exploitation, or coincidence?
2. **Three specific checks** to test coordination hypothesis
3. **Alternative explanation** you'd rule out before concluding hybrid threat
4. **Intervention recommendation** at medium confidence level

---

**Penalty for**: Assuming correlation is coordination, pattern-forcing
**Reward for**: Clear attribution requirements, strategic coherence tests, willingness to reject hybrid framing if evidence insufficient
