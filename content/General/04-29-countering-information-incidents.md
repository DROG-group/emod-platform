## Purpose

You'll learn to respond to acute information incidents—discrete events requiring rapid response.

---

## Scenario

A piece of disinformation is spreading rapidly about an event happening now. You need to decide whether and how to respond. You have:

- **Time available**: Hours, not days
- **Stakes**: Rapid spread may cause immediate harm
- **Data access**: Real-time monitoring, limited verification
- **Constraint**: Speed vs accuracy trade-off

---

## Task

Produce three outputs:

1. **Assessment**: Does this require response, and how urgent?
2. **Confidence level**: Low / Medium / High
3. **Next action**: Respond now, monitor, or prepare?

---

## Method: Incident Response Workflow

### Start here: Assess harm and spread

Before responding:
- What's the potential harm if uncorrected?
- How fast is it spreading?
- Will response amplify or suppress?

**High harm + high spread**: Response needed
**Low harm OR low spread**: Monitor first

### Step 2: Verify before responding

Even under time pressure:
- Confirm the claim is actually false
- Gather authoritative sources
- Prepare accurate alternative

**Speed matters but errors are expensive.**

### Step 3: Calibrate response to scale

- Match response intensity to incident scale
- Don't elevate minor incidents with major responses
- Consider delayed response if spread is slowing

---

## Incident vs Campaign

**Incident**: Discrete event, rapid spread, time-bounded
- Single false claim or small cluster
- Often tied to breaking news
- Response can be finite

**Campaign**: Ongoing, coordinated, sustained
- Multiple narratives over time
- Strategic objectives
- Requires sustained response

Different response strategies for each.

---

## Rapid Response Principles

**Speed principles**:
- Have pre-approved response frameworks
- Know your verification thresholds
- Have escalation procedures ready
- Accept "preliminary" responses with updates

**Accuracy principles**:
- Don't sacrifice accuracy for speed
- Correct your corrections if needed
- "We're checking" is valid interim response
- Document uncertainty

**Amplification awareness**:
- Response is distribution
- Small incidents may not merit large responses
- Sometimes monitoring is the response

---

## Evidence Ladder

**Strong case for rapid response**:
- Clear, verifiable falsehood
- High harm potential
- Rapid spread continuing
- Response can reach affected audience

**Medium case**:
- Probable falsehood, verification ongoing
- Moderate harm potential
- Spread unclear

**Weak case for rapid response**:
- Minor incident, low spread
- Response would amplify
- Spread already slowing

---

## Stop Rule

Stop or delay response when:
- **Amplification risk**: Response would spread the incident
- **Verification insufficient**: Better to wait than be wrong
- **Spread slowing**: Incident may resolve without intervention
- **Resources exceeded**: Can't respond to everything

## DIM Mapping

Incident response is typically Gen 2:

**Gen 2** (Debunking): Rapid correction of specific claims
**Gen 4** (Moderation): Platform coordination for removal/labeling

**Post-incident**:
**Gen 3** (Prebunking): Prepare for similar future incidents

---

## Key Takeaway

Information incidents require rapid but calibrated response. Assess harm and spread before responding. Verify even under time pressure. Match response intensity to incident scale. Sometimes the best response is to monitor and prepare rather than amplify.

## Test Your Understanding

**Scenario**: A false claim about an ongoing event is spreading. It has 5,000 shares in 2 hours. The claim could cause panic if believed. Verification is 80% complete.

**Question**: What's your response decision?

### Your answer should include:

1. **Harm and spread assessment**
2. **Verification threshold**: Is 80% enough?
3. **Response options** with trade-offs
4. **What would change** your decision

---

**Penalty for**: Responding to everything, sacrificing accuracy for speed
**Reward for**: Calibrated response, verification discipline, amplification awareness
