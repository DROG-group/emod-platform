## Purpose

You'll learn to respond to sustained information campaigns—ongoing, coordinated efforts requiring strategic response.

---

## Scenario

You've identified a sustained information campaign with multiple narratives, coordinated actors, and strategic objectives. Single debunks aren't working. You need a strategic response. You have:

- **Time available**: Weeks to months (campaign is ongoing)
- **Stakes**: Campaign is achieving objectives
- **Data access**: Campaign analysis, attribution (partial)
- **Constraint**: Campaign adapts to your responses

---

## Task

Produce three outputs:

1. **Assessment**: What type of campaign and what objectives?
2. **Confidence level**: Low / Medium / High
3. **Next action**: Strategic counter-campaign design

---

## Method: Campaign Response Strategy

### Start here: Understand campaign objectives

What is the campaign trying to achieve?
- Undermine trust in institution?
- Influence specific decision?
- Create division?
- Distract from something else?

**Your response should target the objective, not just the content.**

### Step 2: Map campaign structure

- What narratives are being pushed?
- Through what channels?
- By which actors (attributed/unattributed)?
- How does it adapt to countermeasures?

### Step 3: Design strategic counter-response

- What's your objective? (Stop campaign? Reduce impact? Build resilience?)
- What intervention across DIM generations?
- How do you measure success?
- How do you avoid predictable counter-moves?

---

## Campaign vs Incident Response

**Incident response**: Reactive, rapid, specific
- Counter a specific claim
- Finite intervention
- Success = claim corrected

**Campaign response**: Strategic, sustained, adaptive
- Counter strategic objectives
- Ongoing intervention
- Success = campaign objectives thwarted

**Different timeframes, different strategies.**

---

## Campaign Response Principles

**Strategic patience**:
- Not every narrative needs response
- Campaigns want you reactive and exhausted
- Choose your battles

**Objective focus**:
- Attack the campaign objective, not just claims
- If objective is division, don't respond divisively
- If objective is distraction, don't get distracted

**Adaptation planning**:
- Campaign will adapt to your response
- Plan for counter-moves
- Don't assume static adversary

**Measurement discipline**:
- Define success metrics
- Don't measure activity, measure outcomes
- Know when to claim success or change strategy

---

## Evidence Ladder

**Strong campaign response conditions**:
- Campaign objectives understood
- Strategic counter-objectives defined
- Multi-generation DIM approach
- Measurement framework in place
- Adaptation plan exists

**Medium conditions**:
- Campaign partially understood
- Response strategy emerging
- Some DIM coordination

**Weak conditions**:
- Just reacting to content
- No strategic objective
- No measurement
- Exhausting resources on reactive debunks

---

## Stop Rule

Stop or adjust campaign response when:
- **Campaign ended**: Objectives achieved or abandoned by adversary
- **Strategy failing**: Metrics show no impact
- **Resources exceeded**: Can't sustain strategic response
- **Better approach available**: Different strategy more promising

---

## DIM Mapping

Campaign response typically requires multiple DIM generations:

**Gen 2** (Debunking): Tactical corrections where needed
**Gen 3** (Prebunking): Build resilience to campaign narratives
**Gen 4** (Moderation): Platform coordination on coordinated behavior
**Gen 5** (Interaction): Environmental changes to reduce campaign effectiveness

**Key insight**: Campaign response is not just scaled-up debunking. It requires strategic coordination across interventions.

---

## Key Takeaway

Information campaigns require strategic, sustained responses. Understand campaign objectives. Design counter-strategy targeting those objectives. Coordinate across DIM generations. Measure outcomes, not activity. Plan for adaptation. Know when reactive debunking is feeding the campaign rather than countering it.

---

## Test Your Understanding

**Scenario**: A campaign has been running for months, spreading multiple narratives to undermine trust in elections. Each time you debunk a claim, a new one appears. You're exhausting resources.

**Question**: What's wrong with the current approach, and what would you change?

### Your answer should include:

1. **Diagnosis**: Why debunk-by-debunk isn't working
2. **Objective reframe**: What should counter-strategy target?
3. **DIM coordination**: Which generations would you combine?
4. **Success metric**: How would you know it's working?

---

**Penalty for**: Treating campaigns like repeated incidents
**Reward for**: Strategic objective focus, DIM coordination, adaptation planning
