## Purpose

You'll learn how beliefs sustain themselves even when evidence challenges them—so you can design interventions that account for these dynamics.

---

## Scenario

You've provided clear evidence against a belief. The believer doesn't change their mind. You need to understand why. You have:

- **Time available**: Analysis of persistence mechanisms
- **Stakes**: Understanding persistence prevents wasted interventions
- **Data access**: The belief, the evidence, the response
- **Constraint**: Your evidence may be processed differently than you expect

---

## Task

Produce three outputs:

1. **Assessment**: What mechanisms are sustaining this belief?
2. **Confidence level**: Low / Medium / High
3. **Next action**: Intervention that accounts for persistence?

---

## Method: Persistence Analysis

### Start here: Identify sustaining mechanisms

Beliefs persist through:
- **Information filtering**: Contradicting info doesn't reach believers
- **Interpretation reframing**: Contradicting info is reinterpreted
- **Source dismissal**: Contradiction dismissed based on source
- **Social reinforcement**: Community maintains belief

### Step 2: Assess which mechanisms are active

- Does evidence reach believers? (Filter)
- How is evidence interpreted? (Reframe)
- Who is evidence attributed to? (Source)
- What does community say? (Social)

### Step 3: Design mechanism-aware intervention

Each mechanism suggests different approach:
- Filter → Reach strategies
- Reframe → Framing strategies
- Source → Messenger strategies
- Social → Community strategies

---

## Sustaining Mechanisms

**Confirmation bias filtering**:
- Seek confirming information
- Avoid disconfirming information
- Even when exposed, process selectively

**Reinterpretation**:
- "That's what they want you to think"
- "Exception that proves the rule"
- Contradicting evidence fits the theory

**Source dismissal**:
- "Mainstream media lies"
- "Scientists are paid off"
- Evidence valid only from trusted sources

**Social reinforcement**:
- Community reinforces shared beliefs
- Dissent is punished
- Belief marks membership

---

## Evidence Ladder

**Strong intervention potential**:
- Mechanism identified specifically
- Intervention addresses that mechanism
- Not trying to use logic against identity

**Medium potential**:
- General persistence understood
- Intervention partially addresses mechanisms
- Some mechanism mismatch

**Weak potential**:
- Just presenting more evidence
- Ignoring sustaining mechanisms
- Expecting logic to override identity

---

## Stop Rule

Stop current intervention approach when:
- **Mechanism not addressed**: More evidence won't help if filtering is the problem
- **Backfire occurring**: Intervention strengthening belief
- **Different mechanism needed**: Switch strategies when current one fails

---

## DIM Mapping

Understanding persistence informs DIM choice:

**Gen 2** (Debunking): Works when filtering/source aren't blocking
**Gen 3** (Prebunking): Works before mechanisms activate
**Gen 5** (Interaction): Design to reduce mechanism activation

---

## Key Takeaway

Beliefs sustain themselves through filtering, reinterpretation, source dismissal, and social reinforcement. More evidence doesn't help if these mechanisms are active. Identify which mechanism is operating and design accordingly.

## Test Your Understanding

**Scenario**: Every time you share fact-checks, a believer responds "that's from mainstream media, they're part of the cover-up."

**Question**: What mechanism is sustaining the belief, and what might work better?

### Your answer should include:

1. **Mechanism identification**: Which one is active
2. **Why fact-checks fail** in this case
3. **Alternative approach** that addresses the mechanism

---

**Penalty for**: More facts into the same mechanism
**Reward for**: Mechanism-specific intervention design
