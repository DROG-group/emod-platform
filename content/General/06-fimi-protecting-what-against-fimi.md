## Purpose

You'll learn to specify what you're actually protecting when countering FIMI—because vague goals lead to vague (or overreaching) interventions.

---

## Scenario

Your organisation is tasked with "protecting against FIMI." Before designing interventions, you need to specify what exactly you're protecting. You have:

- **Time available**: Strategy definition phase
- **Stakes**: Unclear protection goals enable mission creep
- **Data access**: Your mandate, threat assessments
- **Constraint**: "Protecting everything" means protecting nothing well

---

## Task

Produce three outputs:

1. **Assessment**: What specific values/institutions are you protecting?
2. **Confidence level**: Low / Medium / High that these are the right priorities
3. **Next action**: Protection strategy matched to specific targets

---

## Method: Protection Target Definition

### Start here: List potential protection targets

What could FIMI undermine?
- Democratic processes (elections, referenda)
- Institutional trust (government, media, science)
- Social cohesion (inter-group relations)
- National security (military, intelligence)
- Public health (health behaviors, trust in medicine)

### Step 2: Prioritise based on mandate

Not everything is your responsibility:
- What's your legal mandate?
- What's your comparative advantage?
- What are others doing?

### Step 3: Define specific protection objectives

Vague: "Protect democracy"
Specific: "Protect election integrity: prevent foreign interference in voter perceptions, process confidence, and participation"

---

## Protection Targets

**Democratic processes**:
- Election integrity (perceptions, confidence, participation)
- Policy deliberation (informed debate)
- Accountability mechanisms (journalism, oversight)

**Institutional trust**:
- Government legitimacy
- Media credibility
- Scientific authority
- Judicial independence

**Social cohesion**:
- Inter-group relations
- Shared factual foundation
- Capacity for collective action

**Security**:
- Military readiness
- Intelligence protection
- Critical infrastructure

**Public health**:
- Health behavior compliance
- Trust in health authorities
- Epidemic response capacity

---

## Specificity Matters

**Why vague goals are problematic**:
- Can't measure success without specific targets
- Can't prioritise resources
- Invites mission creep
- Makes accountability impossible

**Why specific goals help**:
- Focus resources where they matter
- Enable measurement
- Define scope limits
- Support accountability

---

## Evidence Ladder

**Strong protection framework**:
- Specific targets identified
- Prioritisation based on mandate and capacity
- Measurable objectives
- Scope limits defined

**Medium framework**:
- Targets identified but broad
- Some prioritisation
- Limited measurement

**Weak framework**:
- Vague goals ("protect democracy")
- No prioritisation
- No measurement
- Scope undefined (everything/nothing)

---

## Stop Rule

Stop broadening protection scope when:
- **Beyond mandate**: Not your responsibility
- **Beyond capacity**: Can't do it well
- **Better done by others**: Comparative disadvantage
- **Tradeoff with other targets**: Protecting one undermines another

---

## DIM Mapping

Protection targets inform intervention choice:

**Democratic processes**: Gen 3 (prebunk manipulation), Gen 4 (platform coordination during elections)
**Institutional trust**: Gen 5 (design for trustworthy information environment)
**Social cohesion**: Gen 5 (design to reduce divisive amplification)
**Security**: Gen 4 (moderation of security-relevant content)

---

## Key Takeaway

"Protecting against FIMI" is meaningless without specifying what you're protecting. Define specific targets based on mandate and capacity. Prioritise. Define scope limits. This enables measurement, accountability, and focused intervention.

## Test Your Understanding

**Scenario**: Your unit is tasked with "countering FIMI." Budget is limited. You could focus on election protection, institutional trust, or social cohesion.

**Question**: How do you prioritise?

### Your answer should include:

1. **Criteria for prioritisation**
2. **What you'd choose** (with rationale)
3. **What you'd explicitly NOT focus on**
4. **How you'd measure success** for your chosen target

---

**Penalty for**: "Protect everything," vague goals, no prioritisation
**Reward for**: Specific targets, mandate-based scope, measurable objectives
