## Purpose

You'll learn what evidence distinguishes foreign information manipulation from domestic activity—and why "foreign" isn't the only thing that matters.

---

## Scenario

Your team has identified a suspected information operation. Leadership asks: "Is it foreign?" You need to assess what evidence would support that conclusion. You have:

- **Time available**: Investigation phase
- **Stakes**: "Foreign" attribution has political implications
- **Data access**: Account data, content analysis, limited technical indicators
- **Constraint**: Attribution is hard; false positives are costly

---

## Task

Produce three outputs:

1. **Assessment**: What evidence suggests foreign vs domestic origin?
2. **Confidence level**: Low / Medium / High for attribution
3. **Next action**: Is "foreign" attribution necessary for response?

---

## Method: Attribution Analysis

### Start here: Question whether "foreign" matters

Before investigating foreignness:
- Does response require attribution?
- Would you respond differently to domestic version?
- Is "foreign" the key question or is "coordinated/harmful" sufficient?

**Often, you can respond to behavior without proving origin.**

### Step 2: Assess available indicators

**Technical indicators**:
- Infrastructure (servers, domains)
- Account creation patterns
- Timing correlating with foreign time zones
- Language artifacts

**Behavioral indicators**:
- Content alignment with foreign state interests
- Coordination with known foreign accounts
- Tactics matching known foreign operations

**Contextual indicators**:
- Geopolitical context
- Cui bono (who benefits)
- Pattern matching with previous attributed operations

### Step 3: Calibrate confidence

- Technical indicators are stronger but harder to get
- Behavioral indicators are circumstantial
- Contextual indicators are weakest (domestic actors can also benefit)

**High confidence requires multiple independent indicators.**

---

## Attribution Evidence Hierarchy

**Strong indicators** (high confidence contribution):
- Infrastructure directly tied to foreign state
- SIGINT or HUMINT confirmation (classified)
- Admissions/leaks from the operation
- Cross-platform coordination traced to foreign entity

**Medium indicators** (moderate confidence):
- Technical indicators consistent with foreign origin
- Tactics match previous attributed operations
- Coordination with known foreign assets

**Weak indicators** (low confidence, common mistakes):
- Content benefits foreign interests (domestic actors might too)
- Bad grammar or unusual phrasing (not proof of foreign)
- Account claims to be from somewhere (easily faked)
- "Feels foreign" (bias, not evidence)

---

## Why "Foreign" Isn't Everything

**Harm matters regardless of origin**:
- Domestic disinformation can be equally harmful
- Coordination is problematic whoever does it
- Impact on society is the same

**Attribution is expensive**:
- Takes significant resources
- Often impossible without classified access
- May not change response

**Overclaiming risks**:
- Falsely attributing domestic as foreign is costly
- Creates international incidents
- Undermines credibility for real cases

**Alternative framing**: "Coordinated inauthentic behavior" doesn't require foreign attribution to address.

---

## Evidence Ladder

**Strong attribution case**:
- Multiple technical indicators
- Independent corroboration
- Consistent with known capabilities/interests
- Alternative explanations ruled out

**Medium case**:
- Some technical indicators
- Behavioral pattern match
- But alternatives possible

**Weak case** (don't claim foreign):
- Only content/interest alignment
- Only circumstantial evidence
- High false-positive risk

---

## Stop Rule

Stop pursuing foreign attribution when:
- **Not necessary for response**: Can address behavior without origin proof
- **Evidence insufficient**: Better to acknowledge uncertainty
- **Resources better spent**: Investigation cost exceeds attribution value
- **Risk of false positive too high**: Would create more problems than it solves

---

## DIM Mapping

Attribution affects intervention framing:

**If foreign confirmed**: May warrant additional responses (diplomatic, sanctions)
**If uncertain**: Focus on behavior-based response (Gen 4 moderation)
**Either way**: Gen 3 (prebunking) and Gen 5 (interaction) work regardless of origin

---

## Key Takeaway

"Foreign" is one attribute, not the goal. Attribution is hard and often unnecessary for response. Focus on harm + intent + coordination + impact. If you can't prove foreign origin, you can still address harmful coordinated behavior. Don't overclaim attribution—it's better to be uncertain than wrong.

**Question**: What's your attribution assessment?

**Penalty for**: Claiming foreign attribution without strong evidence, treating "who benefits" as proof
