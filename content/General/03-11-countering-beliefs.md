## Purpose

You'll learn what actually works for changing beliefs—and when changing beliefs isn't the right goal.

---

## Scenario

You need to counter a harmful belief. Before designing an intervention, you need to assess whether belief change is achievable and appropriate. You have:

- **Time available**: Intervention design phase
- **Stakes**: Wrong approach wastes resources or backfires
- **Data access**: The belief, audience analysis
- **Constraint**: Not all beliefs are changeable, and some attempts make things worse

---

## Task

Produce three outputs:

1. **Assessment**: Is belief change achievable for this audience?
2. **Confidence level**: Low / Medium / High
3. **Next action**: Pursue belief change or alternative goal?

---

## Method: Belief Change Assessment

### Start here: Assess changeability

Factors affecting changeability:
- How central is belief to identity?
- How long has it been held?
- What social costs to changing?
- Is audience persuadable or committed?

### Step 2: Consider alternative goals

If belief change unlikely:
- **Contain spread**: Prevent new believers
- **Reduce harm**: Mitigate consequences of belief
- **Inoculate others**: Protect those not yet believing
- **Wait for exit**: Support those beginning to doubt

### Step 3: If pursuing belief change

Requirements for success:
- Credible messenger (ideally in-group)
- Frame that doesn't threaten identity
- Alternative that meets same needs
- Sustained engagement, not one-shot

---

## What Research Shows

**Works**:
- Prebunking (before belief forms)
- In-group messengers
- Identity-congruent framing
- Addressing underlying needs
- Patient, sustained engagement

**Doesn't work**:
- Facts alone against identity-beliefs
- Out-group messengers attacking
- One-time correction
- Shame or mockery
- Assuming logical argument suffices

**Backfires**:
- Threats to core identity
- Forced choice between belief and belonging
- Humiliation attempts

---

## Target Audience Segmentation

**Committed believers**: Belief central to identity
- Belief change: Very difficult
- Alternative: Contain, reduce harm, inoculate others

**Moderate believers**: Belief held but not central
- Belief change: Possible with right approach
- Focus: Credible messengers, identity-safe framing

**Curious/undecided**: Exploring the belief
- Belief change: Most achievable
- Focus: Prebunking, information provision

**Former believers**: Already doubting/left
- Belief change: Happened
- Focus: Support, use as messengers

---

## Evidence Ladder

**Strong belief change potential**:
- Audience not identity-committed
- Credible messenger available
- Alternative that meets needs
- Sustained engagement possible

**Medium potential**:
- Mixed audience commitment
- Some credible voices
- Partial alternative available

**Low potential**:
- Deep identity commitment
- No in-group messengers
- No needs-meeting alternative
- One-shot intervention only

---

## Stop Rule

Stop pursuing belief change when:
- **Identity too central**: Switch to containment
- **No credible messenger**: Can't access audience
- **Backfire risk high**: Making it worse
- **Better use of resources**: Focus on persuadables instead

---

## DIM Mapping

Belief change is only one possible goal:

**Gen 2** (Debunking): Works for persuadable, not committed
**Gen 3** (Prebunking): Works before belief forms
**Gen 5** (Interaction): Changes environment so belief doesn't take hold

---

## Key Takeaway

Belief change is possible but conditional. Assess whether the audience is reachable before investing in belief change. For committed believers, contain and inoculate others. For persuadables, use credible messengers and identity-safe framing. Know when to pursue alternatives.

## Test Your Understanding

**Scenario**: An online community holds a harmful belief central to their identity. An outside organisation wants to "debunk" the belief with a public campaign.

**Question**: What's your recommendation?

### Your answer should include:

1. **Changeability assessment** for this audience
2. **Why public debunking** may not work or may backfire
3. **Alternative goals** that might be more achievable
4. **What would make** belief change more feasible

**Penalty for**: Pursuing belief change when containment is better goal
