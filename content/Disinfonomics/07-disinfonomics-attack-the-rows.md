## Purpose

You'll learn to stop subsidising spillovers and design rows that make dumping costs money.

---

## Scenario

You're advising on platform regulation. The debate is stuck between "more moderation" and "free speech." You have:

- **Time available**: 3 hours to draft an alternative framework
- **Stakes**: Policy will shape the information environment for years
- **Data access**: Economic data on platform externalities, regulatory precedents
- **Constraint**: Must be implementable, not utopian

---

## Task

Produce three outputs:

1. **Assessment**: What externalities are currently unpriced?
2. **Confidence level**: Low / Medium / High
3. **Next action**: Design one internalisation mechanism

---

## Screen 1: Four-layer pattern

Every disinformation economy follows this structure:

1. **Private trade** (someone profits)
2. **Spillover bundle** (costs dumped on others)
3. **Avoidance economy** (people pay to escape)
4. **Missing internalisation row** (no one forces cost absorption)

---

## Screen 2: Avoidance economy proves the harm

If people pay to avoid it (security, moderation, PR, policing), it's not hypothetical.

The avoidance economy is your evidence that spillovers are real and costly.

---

## Screen 3: Don't moralise. Reprice.

Markets react to costs, friction, and liability.

"You should stop" doesn't work. "This now costs you" does.

---

## Screen 4: Industrial reach is the lever

Don't tax speech. Target industrial distribution:
- Recommendation algorithms
- Monetised scale
- Boosted reach

Speech is free. Amplification is a service.

---

## Screen 5: Mechanism palette

Tools available:
- **Permits**: License industrial-scale distribution
- **Deposits**: Require bonds against harm
- **Audits**: Mandatory transparency on amplification
- **Levies**: Tax reach that creates externalities
- **Monetisation licensing**: Restrict who can profit from viral content
- **Reach escrow**: Slow viral spread pending review
- **Advertiser duty constraints**: Make advertisers liable for adjacency

---

## Screen 6: Design constraints

Good mechanisms are:
- **Reversible**: Can be undone if wrong
- **Auditable**: Can be checked for bias
- **Proportional**: Scale with harm, not content
- **Not ideology-dependent**: Work regardless of who's in power

---

## Screen 7: Solved ledger (definition)

Not "no lies." A ledger where externalities aren't free to dump.

The goal isn't truth. The goal is a market that prices harm.

---

## Screen 8: Metrics

**Leading indicators** (early signals):
- Reach share of low-quality sources
- Monetisation access for repeat violators
- Friction events (delays, warnings)

**Lagging indicators** (outcome measures):
- Incident frequency
- Harassment volume
- Trust indicators
- Enforcement costs

---

## Screen 9: DIM move

This is the economics under the whole DIM menu:

- Makes **Gen 4** rational (reduce reach where externalities are high)
- Makes **Gen 3** targeted (prebunk where beliefs drive transactions)
- Makes **Gen 5** structural (design environments where harm doesn't pay)

The ledger is the strategy.
