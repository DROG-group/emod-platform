## Purpose

You'll learn to analyse the relationship between platforms and democracy—without defaulting to "platforms bad" or "platforms neutral."

---

## Scenario

A policy debate asks whether platforms help or harm democracy. You need to provide nuanced analysis. You have:

- **Time available**: Preparation for policy briefing
- **Stakes**: Analysis shapes regulatory approach
- **Data access**: Research literature, platform data where available
- **Constraint**: Both "platforms destroy democracy" and "platforms are just neutral tools" are oversimplifications

---

## Task

Produce three outputs:

1. **Assessment**: How do platforms affect democratic processes in this specific context?
2. **Confidence level**: Low / Medium / High
3. **Next action**: What evidence would strengthen or change this assessment?

## Method: Platform-Democracy Analysis

### Start here: Specify the mechanism

"Platforms affect democracy" is too vague. Specify:
- **Which platforms?** (They differ significantly)
- **Which democratic function?** (Information, participation, deliberation, accountability)
- **Through what mechanism?** (Amplification, moderation, data, business model)

### Step 2: Assess competing effects

Platforms have both positive and negative democratic effects:

**Positive**:
- Lower barriers to political participation
- Access to diverse information (potentially)
- Coordination for collective action
- Accountability through visibility

**Negative**:
- Amplification of engaging (often divisive) content
- Concentration of communicative power
- Surveillance and manipulation potential
- Erosion of shared information environment

The net effect depends on context.

### Step 3: Avoid false dichotomies

Neither extreme is accurate:
- Platforms aren't neutral infrastructure (design choices matter)
- Platforms aren't simply destroyers of democracy (they enable participation too)

The question is how to maximise benefits and minimise harms.

## Democratic Functions and Platforms

**Information** (informed citizenry):
- Platforms provide access to more information than ever
- But also to more misinformation
- Algorithmic curation shapes what's visible
- Net effect: Contested, context-dependent

**Participation** (political engagement):
- Lower barriers to entry for political voice
- But also for manipulation and harassment
- Platform design affects who participates
- Net effect: More participation, quality questions

**Deliberation** (reasoned debate):
- Potential for broader conversations
- But incentives favour engagement over reason
- Filter bubbles may limit exposure to opposing views
- Net effect: Mixed evidence, design-dependent

**Accountability** (holding power to account):
- Visibility makes abuses harder to hide
- But also easier to overwhelm with noise
- Platforms themselves are new power centres
- Net effect: Both tool for and subject of accountability

---

## Evidence Ladder

**Strong evidence** (platform effect established):
- Controlled studies showing causal mechanism
- Consistent findings across contexts
- Clear mechanism identified

**Medium evidence** (effect plausible):
- Correlational studies suggesting relationship
- Case studies with plausible mechanisms
- Expert consensus forming

**Weak evidence** (insufficient):
- Anecdotes and high-profile examples
- Intuitive but untested claims
- Conflation of platform with broader trends

---

## Stop Rule

Stop analysis when:
- **Mechanism unclear**: Can't specify how platforms affect democracy
- **Context-dependence overwhelming**: Effect varies too much to generalise
- **Better questions exist**: Specific policy questions more productive than general assessment

---

## DIM Mapping

Platform-democracy analysis informs intervention level:

**Gen 4** (Moderation): Platform policies on political content
**Gen 5** (Interaction): Platform design for democratic health

**Key insight**: Platform effects on democracy aren't fixed—they're shaped by design choices, regulation, and user behavior. The question isn't "good or bad?" but "how to make better?"

---

## Key Takeaway

Platforms aren't neutral tools or democracy destroyers. They have mixed effects depending on design, context, and the specific democratic function. Useful analysis specifies mechanisms rather than making sweeping claims. The goal is optimising design and regulation for democratic health, not declaring platforms good or bad.
