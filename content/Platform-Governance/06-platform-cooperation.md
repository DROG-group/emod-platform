## Purpose

You'll learn how platforms cooperate on information threats—and the governance questions this raises.

---

## Scenario

A coordinated campaign is spreading across multiple platforms. Each platform sees a piece, but no one sees the whole picture. Cooperation could help, but raises questions. You have:

- **Time available**: Operational and policy assessment
- **Stakes**: Effectiveness vs governance concerns
- **Data access**: Knowledge of cooperation mechanisms
- **Constraint**: Cooperation has benefits and risks

---

## Task

Produce three outputs:

1. **Assessment**: When is platform cooperation appropriate?
2. **Confidence level**: Low / Medium / High
3. **Next action**: Recommendations with safeguards

---

## Method: Cooperation Assessment

### Start here: Identify cooperation purpose

Different purposes, different considerations:
- **Threat intelligence**: Sharing indicators of coordinated campaigns
- **Policy coordination**: Aligning on content rules
- **Content sharing**: Notifying of violating content across platforms
- **Research collaboration**: Joint studies of information problems

### Step 2: Evaluate benefits

Cross-platform cooperation can:
- Enable faster response to coordinated campaigns
- Prevent adversaries from simply moving platforms
- Reduce duplication of effort
- Improve detection through shared signals

### Step 3: Assess governance concerns

Cooperation raises questions:
- **Cartel risk**: Platforms acting in concert against users/competition
- **Due process**: Decisions affecting speech made in private
- **Government influence**: State pressure on cooperation decisions
- **Accountability**: Who's responsible for cooperative decisions?

---

## Cooperation Mechanisms

**Industry consortia** (e.g., GIFCT):
- Shared databases of terrorist content hashes
- Joint policy development
- Pros: Efficiency, cross-platform response
- Cons: Limited transparency, membership questions

**Government partnerships**:
- Information sharing during elections
- Coordination on foreign influence
- Pros: State capacity, democratic legitimacy
- Cons: Government overreach risk, global inconsistency

**Ad hoc coordination**:
- Crisis response cooperation
- Rapid escalation channels
- Pros: Flexibility, speed
- Cons: Informal, unaccountable

**Research partnerships**:
- Academic access programs
- Joint research initiatives
- Pros: Independent analysis, public knowledge
- Cons: Access restrictions, conflicts of interest

---

## Governance Safeguards

**For legitimate cooperation**:
- Clear scope limitations
- Transparency about cooperation existence
- Due process for affected users
- Independent oversight
- Competitive markets (so users have alternatives)

**Warning signs**:
- Secret coordination affecting lawful speech
- No appeal mechanisms
- Government mandating specific decisions
- Cooperation used to exclude competitors

---

## Evidence Ladder

**Strong case for cooperation**:
- Clear threat requiring cross-platform response
- Transparent governance structure
- Due process protections in place
- Independent oversight exists

**Medium case**:
- Threat unclear or contested
- Some transparency, limited oversight
- Governance developing

**Weak case for cooperation**:
- Coordination targeting lawful speech
- No transparency or accountability
- Government compulsion without legal basis

---

## Stop Rule

Stop supporting cooperation when:
- **Scope creep**: Expanding beyond original threat focus
- **Accountability absent**: No oversight of cooperative decisions
- **Competition concern**: Cooperation becoming cartel behavior
- **Due process absent**: Users affected without recourse

---

## DIM Mapping

Cooperation enhances Gen 4-5 responses:

**Gen 4** (Moderation): Coordinated enforcement across platforms
**Gen 5** (Interaction): Shared research on design improvements

**Key insight**: Cross-platform threats need cross-platform responses—but with governance proportionate to the power involved.

---

## Key Takeaway

Platform cooperation can improve responses to cross-platform threats. But cooperation between powerful entities requires safeguards: transparency, scope limits, due process, and oversight. The question isn't whether to cooperate but how to do so accountably.
