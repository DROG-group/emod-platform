## Purpose

You'll learn how institutional strategic communication counters information threats—and the legitimacy constraints that should guide it.

---

## Scenario

Your government department wants to "fight disinformation" through strategic communication. Before designing campaigns, you need to define scope and constraints. You have:

- **Time available**: Strategic planning phase (weeks)
- **Stakes**: Effective counter-messaging vs perception of propaganda
- **Data access**: Threat assessments, audience research
- **Constraint**: Government communication carries legitimacy risks

---

## Task

Produce three outputs:

1. **Assessment**: What role should StratCom play in this context?
2. **Confidence level**: Low / Medium / High that StratCom will help
3. **Next action**: Specific recommendations with legitimacy safeguards

---

## Method: StratCom Planning Workflow

### Start here: Define what StratCom is (and isn't)

**StratCom is**:
- Coordinated communication to achieve strategic objectives
- Proactive messaging, not just reactive correction
- Part of broader whole-of-society response

**StratCom is not (or shouldn't be)**:
- Propaganda by another name
- Manipulation of domestic audiences
- The solution to all information problems

### Step 2: Identify legitimate StratCom roles

Government StratCom is appropriate for:
- Providing accurate information on government actions
- Warning about foreign information manipulation
- Supporting media literacy (not controlling content)
- Coordinating with allies on shared threats

Government StratCom is problematic for:
- Targeting domestic political opponents
- Controlling "truth" on contested issues
- Surveillance of citizen speech

### Step 3: Design with legitimacy constraints

Every StratCom action should pass:
- **Transparency test**: Would you defend this if publicised?
- **Reversibility test**: Can this be undone if wrong?
- **Mission creep test**: Does this stay within defined scope?

---

## StratCom Functions

**Proactive communication**:
- Explain government policies and actions
- Build understanding of information threats
- Support resilience through education

**Reactive communication**:
- Rapid response to false claims about government
- Coordination with fact-checkers and platforms
- Crisis communication during information attacks

**Capacity building**:
- Media literacy programs
- Journalist training
- Civil society support

**International coordination**:
- Information sharing with allies
- Coordinated responses to shared threats
- Diplomatic messaging

---

## Legitimacy Constraints

**Essential safeguards**:
- Separation from domestic political operations
- Oversight mechanisms (legislative, independent)
- Transparency about StratCom activities
- Clear legal authority and limits
- Focus on foreign threats, not domestic dissent

**Warning signs of overreach**:
- Targeting domestic political opposition
- Secret domestic operations
- No oversight or accountability
- Defining "disinformation" to include legitimate dissent

---

## Evidence Ladder

**Strong signals** (StratCom appropriate):
- Clear foreign information threat
- Government has information others need
- Legitimate coordination role exists
- Transparency and oversight in place

**Medium signals** (proceed carefully):
- Threat definition is contested
- Domestic and foreign threats blur
- Oversight is limited

**Weak signals** (legitimacy risk high):
- "Disinformation" includes political opponents
- No transparency about activities
- Surveillance of citizens involved
- Mission creep beyond defined scope

---

## Stop Rule

Stop or redesign StratCom when:
- **Legitimacy threshold crossed**: Activity wouldn't survive transparency
- **Mission creep**: Scope expanding beyond original mandate
- **Effectiveness unclear**: Activity continues without evidence it works
- **Better alternatives exist**: Other actors (journalists, civil society) more appropriate

---

## DIM Mapping

StratCom spans DIM generations:

**Gen 2** (Debunking): Rapid response to false claims about government
**Gen 3** (Prebunking): Media literacy and resilience building
**Gen 4** (Moderation): Coordination with platforms (not control)
**Gen 5** (Interaction): Broader information environment improvements

**Key constraint**: Government actors face legitimacy limits that civil society and journalists don't. Design accordingly.

---

## Key Takeaway

Strategic communication is a legitimate tool against information threats—within limits. The line between StratCom and propaganda is transparency, oversight, and scope. Government should provide accurate information, build resilience, and coordinate responses to foreign threats. It should not control truth, target domestic dissent, or operate without accountability.
