## Purpose

You'll learn to design prebunking interventions—not to claim they solve everything, but to use them where they work best.

---

## Scenario

You anticipate a disinformation campaign around an upcoming event. Debunking after the fact has limited effectiveness. You want to inoculate audiences beforehand. You have:

- **Time available**: 2 weeks before anticipated campaign
- **Stakes**: Getting ahead of narratives vs wasting resources if campaign doesn't materialise
- **Data access**: Historical patterns, audience data
- **Constraint**: Prebunking has limits—not a silver bullet

## Method: Prebunking Design Workflow

### Start here: Assess prebunking fit

Prebunking works best when:
- You can anticipate the manipulation technique or narrative
- You can reach the audience before they encounter the disinformation
- The audience is not yet committed to the false belief

**If belief is already established → debunking or counter-narratives**
**If timing is too late → focus on resilience for next time**

### Step 2: Choose prebunking approach

Two main approaches:
- **Technique-based**: Teach manipulation methods (works across topics)
- **Topic-based**: Preview specific false claims (works for anticipated narratives)

**Technique-based is more durable but less specific.**
**Topic-based is more specific but requires accurate prediction.**

### Step 3: Design the inoculation

Key elements:
- **Warning**: Alert that manipulation attempts exist
- **Weakened dose**: Expose to weak form of manipulation
- **Refutation**: Explain why it's manipulative
- **Active engagement**: Have audience practice recognition

---

## How Inoculation Works

**The medical metaphor**:
- Vaccines expose immune system to weakened pathogen
- Immune system builds antibodies
- Later exposure triggers prepared response

**The psychological parallel**:
- Exposure to weakened form of manipulation
- Mind builds "cognitive antibodies"
- Later exposure triggers critical evaluation

**Key research findings**:
- Works across political spectrum
- Effects can last weeks to months
- Technique-based inoculation transfers across topics
- Active engagement strengthens effects

---

## Evidence Ladder

**Strong signals** (prebunking appropriate):
- You can accurately anticipate the manipulation
- You can reach audience before exposure
- Audience is not yet committed to the narrative
- You have time for multi-exposure inoculation

**Medium signals** (may help but limited):
- Prediction is uncertain
- Some audience already exposed
- Single-exposure only possible

**Weak signals** (prebunking may not help):
- Belief already established
- Cannot reach audience before exposure
- Manipulation is novel/unpredictable

---

## Prebunking Limits

**Doesn't fix everything**:
- Effects fade over time (boosters help)
- Works less well on committed believers
- Can't inoculate against everything

**Design matters**:
- Too strong a dose can backfire
- Must be seen before disinformation
- Active engagement beats passive viewing

**Not a replacement for**:
- Systemic changes to information environment
- Platform-level interventions
- Addressing underlying needs narratives serve

---

## Stop Rule

Stop prebunking planning when:
- **Timing is wrong**: Audience already exposed
- **Prediction too uncertain**: Can't anticipate manipulation
- **Resources better spent elsewhere**: Other interventions more cost-effective
- **Already prebunked**: Audience has prior inoculation

---

## DIM Mapping

Prebunking is Gen 3 intervention:

**Gen 3 strengths**: Proactive, scalable, builds resilience
**Gen 3 limits**: Requires prediction, timing, audience reach

**Combine with**:
- **Gen 4** (Moderation): Platform support for prebunking content
- **Gen 5** (Interaction): Environmental changes that support critical thinking
- **Gen 2** (Debunking): For narratives that get through

---

## Key Takeaway

Prebunking is powerful when conditions align: predictable manipulation, reachable audience, sufficient lead time. It's not a replacement for other interventions. Teach techniques (durable) or specific narratives (precise), engage actively, and plan for boosters.
