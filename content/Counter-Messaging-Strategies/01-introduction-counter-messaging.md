## Purpose

You'll learn to choose the right counter-messaging approach—not to default to debunking, but to match intervention to context.

---

## Scenario

A false narrative is gaining traction. Your team's first instinct is to issue a rebuttal. Before you do, you need to assess whether that's the right approach. You have:

- **Time available**: 1 hour to decide on approach
- **Stakes**: Wrong intervention can amplify the problem
- **Data access**: Narrative spread data, audience analysis
- **Constraint**: Resources are limited—can't do everything

---

## Task

Produce three outputs:

1. **Assessment**: Which counter-messaging approach fits this context?
2. **Confidence level**: Low / Medium / High
3. **Next action**: Specific intervention recommendation

---

## Method: Intervention Selection Workflow

### Start here: Assess the lifecycle stage

Where is the narrative in its spread?
- **Early/emerging**: Few people aware, limited spread
- **Growing**: Gaining traction, crossing into mainstream
- **Established**: Widely believed, part of discourse
- **Declining**: Losing relevance, attention moving on

Different stages need different interventions.

### Step 2: Evaluate intervention risks

Before responding, consider:
- Does response amplify reach? (Streisand effect)
- Does response elevate fringe to mainstream?
- Is silence a viable option?

**Key question**: Does our action reduce harm or increase distribution?

### Step 3: Match intervention to context

- **Prebunking**: Best before exposure, builds resilience
- **Debunking**: Best for established claims, when correction can spread
- **Counter-narratives**: Best for belief systems, not individual claims
- **Silence + monitoring**: Best for low-reach content that response would amplify

---

## Counter-Messaging Menu

**Prebunking (Gen 3)**:
- Inoculate before exposure
- Teach manipulation techniques
- Build psychological resistance
- Works best: Before narrative takes hold

**Debunking (Gen 2)**:
- Correct specific false claims
- Provide accurate information
- Works best: When claim is established, audience is reachable

**Counter-narratives**:
- Offer competing story/frame
- Address underlying needs narrative serves
- Works best: When debunking doesn't address why people believe

**Strategic silence**:
- Monitor but don't respond
- Prepare materials for escalation
- Works best: Low-reach content that response would amplify

---

## Evidence Ladder

**Strong signals** (debunking appropriate):
- Narrative is already mainstream
- Audience is persuadable
- Correction can reach same audience as original
- Harm of silence exceeds risk of amplification

**Medium signals** (consider alternatives):
- Narrative is growing but not established
- Audience may be hostile to correction
- Response may elevate the narrative

**Weak signals** (debunking may backfire):
- Narrative is fringe/low-reach
- Your response would be primary distribution
- Audience is deeply committed to belief

---

## Stop Rule

Stop counter-messaging when:
- **Amplification risk too high**: Response does more harm than silence
- **Audience unreachable**: Better to inoculate future audiences
- **Resources exceeded**: Can't sustain correction at scale
- **Narrative declining**: Let it die naturally

---

## DIM Mapping

Counter-messaging spans DIM generations:

**Gen 2** (Debunking): Reactive correction after spread
**Gen 3** (Prebunking): Proactive inoculation before exposure
**Gen 4** (Moderation): Platform-level responses
**Gen 5** (Interaction): Environmental changes that reduce manipulation susceptibility

**Key insight**: Detection (knowing there's a problem) is not the intervention. Choosing the right response is the intervention.

---

## Key Takeaway

Counter-messaging is not just debunking. Match your intervention to the context: lifecycle stage, audience reachability, amplification risk. Sometimes the best response is strategic silence. Ask: does our action reduce harm or increase distribution?
