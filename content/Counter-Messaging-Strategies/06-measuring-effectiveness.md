## Purpose

You'll learn to measure whether counter-messaging works—acknowledging that measurement is hard and vanity metrics are tempting.

---

## Scenario

You've run a counter-messaging campaign. Leadership asks: "Did it work?" You need to provide honest assessment. You have:

- **Time available**: Post-campaign evaluation period
- **Stakes**: Future resource allocation depends on demonstrated effectiveness
- **Data access**: Campaign metrics, some outcome data
- **Constraint**: Proving causation is hard; many factors affect beliefs

---

## Task

Produce three outputs:

1. **Assessment**: What can you actually measure, and what does it tell you?
2. **Confidence level**: Low / Medium / High that your intervention caused observed changes
3. **Next action**: Recommendations for future measurement

---

## Method: Effectiveness Measurement Workflow

### Start here: Define what "worked" means

Before measuring, clarify objectives:
- **Reach**: Did the message get to target audience?
- **Engagement**: Did people interact with it?
- **Belief change**: Did beliefs actually shift?
- **Behavior change**: Did actions change?
- **Harm reduction**: Did the problem decrease?

These are very different outcomes requiring different measurement.

### Step 2: Acknowledge what you can't measure

Be honest about limits:
- Correlation isn't causation
- Many factors affect beliefs simultaneously
- Control groups are often impossible
- Self-reported belief change is unreliable

**Temptation**: Report vanity metrics that look good but don't prove effectiveness.

### Step 3: Design for learning, not just reporting

Better than "did it work?":
- What aspects worked better than others?
- For which audiences?
- What would we do differently?

---

## Measurement Hierarchy

**What you can measure (easier → harder)**:

**Reach** (easiest):
- Impressions, views, distribution
- Tells you: People were exposed
- Doesn't tell you: Whether it mattered

**Engagement**:
- Likes, shares, comments, time spent
- Tells you: People interacted
- Doesn't tell you: Whether beliefs changed

**Recall/comprehension**:
- Do people remember the message?
- Tells you: Message was processed
- Doesn't tell you: Whether it affected beliefs

**Attitude/belief change** (harder):
- Survey before/after with control group
- Tells you: Beliefs shifted
- Attribution to your intervention still uncertain

**Behavior change** (hardest):
- Did people act differently?
- Tells you: Real-world impact
- Causation very hard to establish

---

## Honest Reporting

**Vanity metrics** (tempting but misleading):
- "10 million impressions!" (but who saw it?)
- "500% engagement increase!" (from tiny base?)
- "Widely shared!" (by whom?)

**Honest metrics** (harder but useful):
- Reach within target audience specifically
- Engagement quality (shares by persuadables, not just supporters)
- Pre/post belief measurement with controls
- Comparison to counterfactual (what would have happened without intervention?)

**Best practice**: Report what you measured honestly, including what you couldn't measure.

---

## Evidence Ladder

**Strong evidence** (intervention likely worked):
- Randomised controlled trial showing belief change
- Pre/post measurement with plausible control
- Consistent effects across multiple measures
- Mechanism (why it worked) is clear

**Medium evidence** (possibly worked):
- Pre/post change without control group
- Target audience reached with engagement
- Anecdotal evidence of impact

**Weak evidence** (can't conclude it worked):
- Reach and engagement metrics only
- No belief/behavior measurement
- No comparison to counterfactual

---

## Stop Rule

Stop claiming effectiveness when:
- **Metrics don't support it**: Reach ≠ impact
- **No comparison**: Can't know what would have happened otherwise
- **Selection bias**: Only measured people who engaged
- **Attribution unclear**: Many factors changed simultaneously

---

## DIM Mapping

Measurement informs DIM selection:

**Gen 2** (Debunking): Measure correction reach, belief persistence
**Gen 3** (Prebunking): Measure resilience to subsequent exposure
**Gen 4** (Moderation): Measure content reduction, spread changes
**Gen 5** (Interaction): Measure environmental changes, long-term trends

**Key insight**: Different interventions need different measurement. Don't apply debunking metrics to prebunking campaigns.

---

## Key Takeaway

Measuring counter-messaging effectiveness is hard. Resist vanity metrics. Be honest about what you can and cannot measure. Design for learning, not just reporting. The question isn't just "did it work?" but "what did we learn about what works?"

## Test Your Understanding

**Scenario**: Your prebunking campaign reached 2 million people, got 50,000 engagements, and subsequent surveys showed the target audience was 10% more skeptical of manipulation techniques. Leadership asks if the campaign was successful.

**Question**: How do you report this honestly?

### Your answer should include:

1. **What the metrics tell you** (and don't tell you)
2. **Attribution challenges**: Can you claim the skepticism was caused by your campaign?
3. **What you'd measure differently** next time
4. **Honest summary statement** for leadership

---

**Penalty for**: Vanity metric inflation, claiming causation without evidence
**Reward for**: Honest attribution limits, learning orientation, measurement improvement plans
